# Support MyBuoy

## What MyBuoy is

MyBuoy is a GPS application for hobbies. It is not a route guide and probably never will be: it
computes no itinerary, reads no traffic, offers no alternative route, does not speak, and exists
in one language only. Others do all of that remarkably well, with servers and means far beyond
this project; to get from one point to another, using them is still the right reflex.

MyBuoy answers a different question. Not "how do I get there", but "where was I, and how do I
get back". It is the question a fisherman, a coastal sailor and a walker all ask: where the last
fish bit and where the shoal is probably still holding, where the car is parked, the corner of
the wood with the chanterelles, the bank with the daffodils, the path you want to measure or run
again.

So the central gesture is this one: drop a mark, and remove it, in two taps, with no form to
fill and no account to create. Everything else is built around it: the distance and bearing to
the mark you aim at, the alarm that warns you when you close in on it or leave its zone, the
sights that measure before you drop, and a map that keeps working with no network, because out
at sea and deep in the woods there is none.

The application also knows where it is. On the water the avatar becomes a boat, and the official
marks (lateral buoys, cardinals, isolated dangers) appear with their nautical chart symbols.
On land it walks, rides or drives depending on your speed. And the heading it shows is a real
magnetic heading, corrected for the local declination and, if you want, for the deviation curve
of your own compass.

What is still to come is sharing. The way some road applications let you report a hazard, MyBuoy
will let you share your marks, without dictating what they mean. A shoal, a quiet anchorage, a
viewpoint, a path that appears on no map. What deserves to be flagged is for you to decide.

## Why the project needs support

MyBuoy is written by one developer, on evenings and weekends, and paid for out of one pocket.
The application itself costs nothing and never will: there is no advertising, no tracking, no
account to create, no feature held behind a paywall. What it does cost is real and recurring:
hosting for the offline map data, the developer account fees, the phones and tablets it has to
be tested on, and above all the hours that go into it instead of somewhere else.

Contributing is what keeps that arrangement possible: the money buys time and infrastructure,
nothing else. It does not buy influence over what the application does with your position,
which stays on your device.

## How to contribute

**With code.** The full source is public and the project accepts pull requests. Start with the
[contribution guide](CONTRIBUTING.md), which covers the licence, the Contributor License
Agreement and the workflow; then open an issue describing what you want to change before you
write it. It avoids two people rebuilding the same thing twice.

**By testing it.** This is the contribution the project is shortest on. MyBuoy is used on foot
and at sea, in conditions that no emulator reproduces: a bad GPS fix in a marina, a mark that
disappears at the edge of a chunk, an alarm that fires late. Reporting precisely what you saw,
where, and at what speed is worth more than a donation.

**With money.** Through [GitHub Sponsors](https://github.com/sponsors/BlackDuckyGB), once or every month.

**By talking about it.** Telling one other sailor or walker that the application exists costs
nothing and is how every user so far has arrived.

## What you get in return

Nothing is reserved for contributors, and nothing ever will be: whatever a contribution adds to
the application is then available to everyone. What you get is not a privilege, it is the right
to decide what gets added.

From €10 you pick a vehicle skin and have it drawn the way you want it, in whichever category
suits you: boat, slow, middle or fast. From €20 it is a mark of your choosing. From €25 you get
both. Whatever you choose, the result ships inside the application and every user can pick it.
And whatever the amount, your handle joins the thanks list on the "Support the development"
page, unless you would rather stay anonymous.

There is one limit, and it is not negotiable: nothing that harms anyone. No pornography,
nothing depicting minors, no political or religious campaigning, no symbol designed to wound,
the flag of a terrorist organisation for instance. A place of worship used as a landmark is a
different matter and perfectly ordinary: a church, a chapel or a mosque can be drawn without
trouble. What is ruled out is proselytising and offence, not heritage.

## Where the money goes

Every contribution is spent on the application itself. The [roadmap](ROADMAP.md) shows the budget
available and which tasks are waiting for funding.

Most of it goes into infrastructure: the servers the coming multi-user mode will need (the one
that will let you share your marks with other people), and building, then hosting, the offline
maps, whose volume is counted in gigabytes. Then comes the hardware: the computers the project
is built on, and the phones it has to be checked on, across several generations of Android.
Then the licences for development tools and the paid technical services without which some
features would simply not exist.

And the rest funds the time. It is the least spectacular line and the one that decides the
most: every hour spent on MyBuoy is an hour taken from somewhere else, and it is that hour
which sets the pace at which releases come out.
